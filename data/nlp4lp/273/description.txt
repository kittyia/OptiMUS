A state is planning its electricity capacity for the next several years. The state has a forecast of the demand for electricity each year, which is presumed to be accurate. Currently, there are existing oil-fired power plants that will continue to operate and provide some of the needed electricity.
To meet the growing demand, the state is considering building either coal-fired or nuclear power plants. Building coal plants costs a certain amount per unit of capacity, and similarly, building nuclear plants has its own cost per unit of capacity.
However, there are limitations. For political and safety reasons, the state has decided that nuclear power should not exceed a certain percentage of the total electricity capacity. Additionally, coal plants have a certain lifespan, after which they will no longer be operational, and the same goes for nuclear plants.
The goal is to figure out the most cost-effective plan to expand electricity capacity by deciding how much to invest in coal and nuclear plants each year, while adhering to the constraints on nuclear power and considering the lifespan of each type of plant.
