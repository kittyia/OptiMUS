### Why this is a constraint

The problem states that:
- Production requirements must be satisfied each year for each manpower category.
- Short-time workers count as **0.5 of a full-time worker** toward meeting requirements.
- Any workforce in excess of requirements is captured through the **overmanning variable**, which incurs cost.

This directly defines a **workforce balance/coverage constraint**. In every year \( i \) and category \( k \), the effective workforce (after accounting for:
- previous workforce minus wastage,
- plus recruitment,
- minus redundancies,
- minus short-time adjustment (since they count as 0.5),
- etc.)

must cover the required manpower.

A typical formulation would look like:

\[
\text{EffectiveWorkforce}_{k,i} = \text{Requirement}_{k,i} + \text{Overmanning}_{k,i}

where:

\[
\text{EffectiveWorkforce}_{k,i}
= \text{FullTime}_{k,i} + 0.5 \cdot \text{Short}_{k,i}
\]

This is not optional — without it, the model would not enforce feasibility of meeting production demand. The overmanning variable only makes sense *because* it captures surplus relative to this requirement constraint.

### Conclusion
