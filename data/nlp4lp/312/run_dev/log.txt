The problem explicitly states:

- All current workers have **more than one year** of service.
- Recruitment occurs each year.
- Redundancy decisions are made each year.

This means workforce size is not static — it evolves over time according to:

1. Survival after wastage (different rates depending on tenure),
2. New recruits (who enter the “less than one year” category),
3. Redundancies (which reduce the workforce).

- You cannot correctly compute how many workers are available in future years.
- You cannot apply the correct wastage rates.
- You cannot ensure production requirements are met.
- The optimization would ignore the dynamic structure of the labor system.

This is not optional bookkeeping — it is a **core flow-balance constraint** that links decisions across years.

### Structurally, this is a workforce balance constraint

\[
\text{Workforce}_{k,i}

\text{Surviving workforce from year } i-1
+
\text{Recruits}_{k,i}
-
\text{Redundancies}_{k,i}
\]

with survival computed using:
- `lessonewaste_k` for workers hired in the previous year,
- `moreonewaste_k` for all others.

This is a **dynamic inventory/flow conservation constraint**, and it is fundamental to the model.
