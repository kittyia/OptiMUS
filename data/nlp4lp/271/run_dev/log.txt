The two constraints serve different and essential purposes:

1. **Resource constraints** ensure that, for each raw material \( i \), total consumption across all products does not exceed availability. These constraints are indexed over raw materials \( i \) and involve all decision variables \( x[j] \).

2. **Non-negativity constraints** ensure that production quantities \( x[j] \) are physically meaningful (no negative production). These are indexed over products \( j \) and are independent of the resource constraints.

There is:
- **No redundancy**: Neither constraint type implies the other.
- **No removable constraint**: Both are required for a well-defined and feasible production model.
- **No combinable constraints**: They apply over different index sets and represent fundamentally different restrictions (capacity vs. domain constraints).
