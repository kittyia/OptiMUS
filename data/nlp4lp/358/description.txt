Background: Transit monitoring is a preventive approach used to identify possible cases of human trafficking before exploitation while an individual is in transit or before crossing a border. Transit monitoring is often conducted by nongovernmental organizations (NGOs) that train staff to identify and intercept suspicious activity. We want to develop a system that uses data envelopment analysis to help decision makers evaluate the performance of these station at intercepting potential human trafficking victims given the amount of resources available (staff, etc.) and make specific operational improvement recommendations.
    
Objective: Minimize the scaled sum of weighted inputs minus a scale factor $v$

Parameters:
- J (|J|): Set of decision-making units
- M (|M|): Set of inputs - number of staff, staff test scores, hours worked by staff
- N (|N|): Set of outputs - count of IRFs, count of VIFs, IRF completeness, VIF completeness, percentage of correct instances of trafficking
- x_m_j (|M|, |J|): Amount of input $m \in M$ from unit $j \in J$
- y_n_j (|N|, |J|): Amount of output $n \in N$ from unit $j \in J$
- w_m (|M|): Weight given to input $m \in M$
- u_n (|N|): Weight given to output $n \in N$
- v (): Scale factor

Constraints:
- The sum of the weights given to the output times the amount of output from all $y_n_j$ should be 1
- The difference of the sum of the weights given to the inputs times the inputs minus the sum of the weight given to the outputs times the outputs minus the scale factor $v$ should be more than or equal for 0 for all j
