A wholesaler on a B2B marketplace sells two components, A and B. For each component, the platform must choose exactly one selling price from a finite list of candidate prices. For each component and candidate price, the corresponding demand is known in advance. The platform must respect a minimum gross margin requirement: for every component, the chosen price must be at least the unit acquisition cost plus a specified minimum per‑unit margin. The platform orders an integer quantity of each component and pays a fixed ordering cost whenever it decides to order a positive quantity of that component.

Component A has a unit acquisition cost of 5 and a minimum per unit margin of 2, so its chosen price must be at least 7. The candidate prices for Component A are 7, 8, and 10. The demand for Component A is 120 units at price 7, 100 units at price 8, and 70 units at price 10. Component B has a unit acquisition cost of 3 and a minimum margin of 1.5, so its price must be at least 4.5. The candidate prices for Component B are 5, 6, and 7. The demand for Component B is 200 units at price 5, 160 units at price 6, and 130 units at price 7.

The fixed ordering costs are 180 for Component A and 150 for Component B. The platform faces a global budget on acquisition costs: the total spending on purchasing units of all components, computed as the sum of acquisition cost times quantity ordered for each component, cannot exceed 1,500.

For each component and candidate price, introduce a binary variable indicating whether that price is chosen, an integer variable for the order quantity of that component, and variables for the units actually sold at each candidate price. Exactly one price must be chosen for each component. For any component and chosen price, the quantity sold cannot exceed the corresponding demand, and the total amount sold of that component cannot exceed its ordered quantity. A binary variable links the decision to order a component with its order quantity so that the quantity is zero whenever the component is not ordered. The candidate price lists for the two components are constructed so that every possible price already satisfies the minimum margin condition. The objective is to maximize total profit, defined as total sales revenue minus acquisition and fixed ordering costs, while respecting the overall acquisition budget of 1,500.
