A wholesaler supplies two products to two online retailers. For each product, the wholesaler sets a wholesale price and offers each retailer a menu of recommended retail prices. For every combination of product, retailer, and candidate retail price, the demand at that price is known in advance. Each retailer decides whether to list a given product and, if so, which retail price from the menu to use. For each product and retailer, introduce binary decision variables that indicate whether that product is listed at a particular price, and continuous variables for the number of units sold at that price.

The wholesaler must decide how many units of each product to produce or reserve to support all retailers, and there is a global production capacity that limits the total number of units that can be produced across both products. Units sold are billed on consignment: the retailer only pays the wholesale price for units actually sold, while any reserved but unsold units remain with the wholesaler and incur a holding cost per unit.

Product 1 has a wholesale price of 6, a production cost of 4 per unit, and a holding cost of 0.7 per leftover unit. It can be listed at retail prices 10 or 12. For Product 1 at Retailer R1, the demand is 80 units at price 10 and 60 units at price 12. For Product 1 at Retailer R2, the demand is 50 units at price 10 and 35 units at price 12.

Product 2 has a wholesale price of 9, a production cost of 6 per unit, and a holding cost of 1.0 per leftover unit. It can be listed at retail prices 15 or 18. For Product 2 at Retailer R1, the demand is 40 units at price 15 and 28 units at price 18. For Product 2 at Retailer R2, the demand is 55 units at price 15 and 38 units at price 18.

The wholesaler has a total production capacity of at most 200 units across Product 1 and Product 2. For each product and retailer, at most one price can be active, meaning the retailer either does not list the product at all or lists it at exactly one price. The number of units sold for any product, at any retailer, and at any price cannot exceed the corresponding demand. For each product, the sum of units sold across all retailers and prices cannot exceed the number of units reserved for that product. The total reserved quantity across both products cannot exceed the wholesaler’s global capacity. The objective is to maximize the wholesaler’s profit, defined as wholesale revenue from actual sales minus production and holding costs.
