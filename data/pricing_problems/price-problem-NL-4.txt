A supermarket is planning weekly prices for one coffee product over the next 13 weeks. In each week, the manager must choose exactly one selling price from a fixed price ladder with four allowed values: 3.49 dollars as the regular price, and 3.19, 2.99, and 2.79 dollars as three promotional prices. The store pays a unit procurement cost of 2.10 dollars every week, and it wants to choose weekly prices to maximize total profit over the 13-week planning horizon. Weekly profit is the unit margin, which is selling price minus unit cost, multiplied by that week’s demand.

The retailer is not allowed to promote too often. Across all 13 weeks, it can run promotions in at most 3 weeks, where a promotion means using any discounted price rather than the regular price. The retailer also wants separation between promotions, so in any 3 consecutive weeks, at most 1 week can be promotional. The retailer must choose one and only one price level in each week. It cannot skip a week, and it cannot assign two prices to the same week.
​
Demand is deterministic and depends not only on the current week’s price, but also on recent prices from earlier weeks because promotions can create a post-promotion dip effect. In this training instance, demand depends on the current week’s price, the previous week’s price, and the price from two weeks earlier. Use the following baseline demand factors for the 13 weeks: week 1 is 6200, week 2 is 6150, week 3 is 6300, week 4 is 6400, week 5 is 6500, week 6 is 6550, week 7 is 6600, week 8 is 6700, week 9 is 6800, week 10 is 6900, week 11 is 7000, week 12 is 7100, and week 13 is 7200. For the two weeks before the planning horizon starts, assume the product was sold at the regular price of 3.49 dollars in both weeks.
​
To make the demand model explicit, use this concrete nonlinear demand rule: demand in week t equals the baseline factor for week t, multiplied by the current week’s price raised to the power negative 3.277, multiplied by last week’s price raised to the power 0.518, and multiplied by the price two weeks ago raised to the power 0.465. This keeps the training sample close to the paper’s setting, where demand can depend on current and lagged prices and the overall POP is a nonlinear binary optimization model.
​
The decision is therefore to choose one price level for each of the 13 weeks so that total profit is as large as possible, while satisfying the promotion limit and the separation rule.
