In fact, it explicitly states that:

- Demand must be fulfilled.
- The yard has an initial number of containers.
- The yard cannot exceed maximum capacity.
- The yard must end with zero containers.

Given the inventory balance equation:

\[
I_t = I_{t-1} + \text{amount}_t - \text{demand}_t
\]

Without explicitly enforcing:

\[
I_t \ge 0 \quad \forall t
\]

the model could mathematically drive inventory negative to reduce unloading or crane costs, which would violate the physical meaning of the system.

Even if demand fulfillment is modeled carefully, the inventory non-negativity constraint (or equivalently, a lower bound of 0 on the inventory variable) is structurally required in a correct formulation.

So yes — this is absolutely a real constraint and should be explicitly modeled (typically as a variable lower bound).
